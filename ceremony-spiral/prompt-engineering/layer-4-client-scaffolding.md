# Layer 4: Client Scaffolding (Pilot Customer Journey Mapping)

**Purpose:** Guide pilot customer interactions with structural tension mapping and ceremonial respect.

**When to use:** When Jerry (or other client success lead) engages pilot customers for onboarding, relationship building, or deployment planning.

---

## The Prompt Template

```markdown
# Pilot Customer Journey Mapping Prompt

Context: We're engaging with [ORGANIZATION NAME] as a potential pilot customer for Ceremony Spiral.

## Customer Profile

**Organization:** [NAME]
**Type:** [Indigenous-led org / Enterprise / Government / NGO]
**Primary Contact:** [NAME, ROLE]
**Context:** [What they do, where they operate]

## Current State Analysis

**Their Desired Outcome:** (Where do they want to be?)
[What they've told us they're trying to achieve]

**Their Current Reality:** (Where are they now?)
[What challenges they're facing, what's not working]

**Structural Tension:** (The gap)
[The dynamic between where they are and where they want to be]

## Their Ceremony & Culture

**Existing Practices:**
[What ceremonies, traditions, or cultural practices already exist in their org?]

**Decision-Making Processes:**
[How do they make decisions? Who needs to be involved?]

**Relationship Values:**
[What do they value in partnerships?]

---

Generate customer journey map that:

## 1. Honors Their Ceremony & Culture
How do we respect their existing practices?
How does Ceremony Spiral support (not replace) what they do?
Where do we adapt our approach to fit their culture?

## 2. Maintains Our Relational Principles
How do we ensure bidirectional benefit (not extraction)?
What do we learn from them (not just what they get from us)?
How do we maintain accountability to them?

## 3. Shows How Ceremony Spiral Helps Them Advance
Specific ways Ceremony Spiral closes their structural tension
Not generic features, but tailored to their actual situation
Concrete examples from their work

## 4. Includes Check-In Points for Accountability
Week 1: [What we confirm together]
Week 4: [What we assess together]
Week 8: [What we learn together]
Ongoing: [How relationship continues]

## 5. Anticipates Resistance/Questions
What concerns might they have?
What questions will they ask?
How do we address authentically (not just overcome objections)?

## 6. Defines Success Together
What does success look like for them?
What does success look like for us?
How do we ensure both are served?
```

## Example Usage

### Scenario: Indigenous-Led Community Development Org

```
Pilot Customer Journey Mapping Prompt:

Organization: Four Directions Community Development Society
Type: Indigenous-led non-profit
Primary Contact: Elder Mary Whitehorse, Executive Director
Context: They manage multiple community development projects (housing, education, cultural programs) across three First Nations communities in BC. Currently using spreadsheets and email to coordinate.

Desired Outcome: Transparent project tracking that honors community relationships and traditional decision-making (Elder council approval required for major decisions)

Current Reality: Information scattered across systems; hard to see project status; community members don't feel included in progress updates; Elder council doesn't have visibility they need to make informed decisions

Structural Tension: Need modern project management efficiency BUT must honor traditional governance and community inclusion (Western PM tools feel transactional and exclude people)

Their Ceremony & Culture:
- Elder council meets monthly; all major decisions require their blessing
- Community gatherings happen seasonally; important announcements made in person
- Consensus decision-making; individual staff don't make unilateral choices
- Seven Generations principle guides all work

Generate customer journey map following Layer 4 template.
```

### Expected Output

```markdown
## Pilot Customer Journey Map: Four Directions Community Development Society

### 1. Honoring Their Ceremony & Culture

**Existing Practices We Honor:**

- **Elder Council Monthly Meeting:** Ceremony Spiral can generate monthly summary reports automatically, formatted for Elder council review. These reports don't replace the sacred meeting; they prepare the ground for better discussions.

- **Seasonal Community Gatherings:** Chart views can be exported as printable posters for community halls. Digital isn't the primary communication; it supports in-person ceremony.

- **Consensus Decision-Making:** Ceremony Spiral's "perspectives honored" feature aligns with their consensus model. Every chart entry asks: "Whose voices informed this?" This isn't bureaucratic; it's how they already work.

- **Seven Generations:** Long-term view settings in Ceremony Spiral. Projects can track: "How does this serve future generations?" Not just quarterly metrics.

**How We Adapt:**

- No push notifications (too extractive; doesn't honor their slower, thoughtful pace)
- Elder council approval workflow built into ceremony templates
- Community language options (not just English)
- Offline-capable (internet connectivity isn't reliable in all three communities)

### 2. Maintaining Our Relational Principles

**Bidirectional Benefit:**

What They Get:
- Project visibility that serves their governance needs
- Tool that respects their decision-making culture
- Training and ongoing support
- Connection to other Indigenous-led orgs using Ceremony Spiral

What We Get:
- Learning about how Indigenous governance principles translate to technology
- Real-world validation of ceremonial workflows
- Relationships with their community network
- Case study showing authentic Indigenous partnership (not extractive)

**Accountability:**

- Monthly check-in with Elder Mary: "Is this tool serving you well?"
- Quarterly review with Elder Council: "Does this honor your processes?"
- Community feedback channel: Anyone can share concerns directly
- Commitment: If relational accountability breaks down, we stop deployment (not just "fix bugs")

### 3. How Ceremony Spiral Helps Them Advance

**Closing Their Structural Tension:**

**Problem:** Elder Council needs visibility to make informed decisions, but current spreadsheets don't show the full picture.

**Solution:** Ceremony Spiral generates visual chart showing:
- Current Reality of each project (where we are)
- Desired Outcome (where we're going)
- Structural Tension (the path forward)
- Perspectives Honored (who contributed to this understanding)

Elder Council can see at a glance: "Which projects are advancing well? Which need support? Who's involved in each?"

**Specific Example from Their Work:**

**Housing Project (Current):**
- Scattered info: Building permits in email, budget in spreadsheet, community feedback in meeting notes
- Elder Council asks: "Where are we with the housing project?" → Staff scrambles to compile update

**Housing Project (With Ceremony Spiral):**
- All info in one chart
- Current Reality: "Permits approved, budget 60% allocated, community expressed concerns about location"
- Desired Outcome: "12 new homes by Fall 2026, community feels included in decisions"
- Structural Tension: "Need to address location concerns before construction starts"
- Perspectives: "Elders, youth council, construction team, funding partner"

Elder Council sees complete picture instantly. Their monthly meeting focuses on decisions, not information gathering.

### 4. Check-In Points for Accountability

**Week 1: Initial Setup**

Together we:
- Review their existing project list
- Choose 2-3 pilot projects to start with
- Set up Elder Council approval workflow
- Confirm: Does this feel respectful? Or extractive?

Success criteria: Elder Mary says "This feels like it could work for us"

**Week 4: First Elder Council Meeting**

Together we:
- Generate first monthly report for Elder Council
- Attend their meeting (if invited) to observe how report is used
- Gather feedback: What helped? What got in the way?
- Adjust: Make changes based on their input

Success criteria: Elder Council finds report useful for decision-making

**Week 8: Community Check-In**

Together we:
- Present Ceremony Spiral at community gathering (if appropriate)
- Gather community feedback directly (not filtered through staff)
- Ask: "Does this honor your involvement? Or does it feel like another system imposed on you?"
- Adjust: Make changes based on community voice

Success criteria: Community members report feeling more included in project progress

**Ongoing: Quarterly Reviews**

Together we:
- Review all projects quarterly with Elder Council
- Ask: "Is this still serving you well?"
- Learn: "What are we learning together?"
- Evolve: "How should this tool grow with your needs?"

Success criteria: Partnership deepens over time; doesn't plateau

### 5. Anticipating Resistance/Questions

**Likely Questions:**

**Q: "We've tried project management software before. It didn't respect our ways. How is this different?"**

A (Honest): "You're right to be skeptical. Many PM tools are designed for corporate speed, not community wisdom. Ceremony Spiral is different because it's built on Indigenous principles from the ground up—Two-Eyed Seeing, Four Directions, relational accountability. But words are easy. We're asking you to pilot this precisely so you can tell us if we're actually living up to those principles or just using the language. If it doesn't honor your ways, we want to know so we can adjust—or if needed, acknowledge this isn't the right fit."

**Q: "What happens to our community data? Who owns it?"**

A (Honest): "You do. Period. Ceremony Spiral is built on OCAP® principles (Ownership, Control, Access, Possession). Your data stays in your COAIA instance, under your control. We don't extract it for our purposes. When we learn from working with you, we ask permission before sharing anything publicly. If you end this pilot, your data stays yours; you don't lose access."

**Q: "This sounds expensive. We have limited budget."**

A (Honest): "For pilot phase, there's no cost. We're learning together. Long-term pricing will be structured so Indigenous-led organizations like yours have priority access at sustainable rates. We're not building this to extract maximum revenue from communities; we're building it to serve communities while creating sustainable business. If cost becomes a barrier to continuation, we talk about it openly—we don't want you using tools that don't serve you just because you already invested time."

**Likely Resistance:**

**Resistance: "Our Elders aren't comfortable with technology."**

Response: "Then we adapt. Can Ceremony Spiral generate paper reports that honor their preferences? Can younger staff use the digital tools while Elders engage in the way that's comfortable for them? Technology should serve Elders' wisdom, not replace it."

**Resistance: "We're already overwhelmed. We don't have time to learn new systems."**

Response: "Fair. We don't want to add to overwhelm. What if we handle initial setup? What if we provide ongoing support so it doesn't fall on your team? If Ceremony Spiral doesn't save you time within 8 weeks, we stop the pilot. We're not interested in tools that create more work."

### 6. Defining Success Together

**Success for Four Directions Society:**

- Elder Council has visibility they need for informed decisions
- Community members feel more included in project progress
- Staff time is freed up (less scrambling to compile updates)
- Traditional governance processes are supported, not undermined
- Projects advance more smoothly because everyone has shared understanding

**Measurement:**
- Elder Council survey: "Is this helping your decision-making?" (Target: 8/10+)
- Community feedback: "Do you feel more included?" (Target: Majority yes)
- Staff time saved: At least 4 hours/week (measured)
- Projects: At least one project advances faster with Ceremony Spiral than without

**Success for Ceremony Spiral:**

- We learn how Indigenous governance translates to technology
- We validate that ceremonial workflows actually work at scale
- We build deep relationship with Four Directions and their network
- We gather authentic testimonial (not transactional: "We used this tool"; but relational: "This partnership honored our ways")
- We position for broader Indigenous community deployment

**Measurement:**
- Learning documented: Monthly "What we learned" reports
- Relationship depth: Are we invited to community gatherings? Do they introduce us to other orgs?
- Testimonial quality: Does Elder Mary's testimonial reflect genuine partnership?
- Network effect: Do other Indigenous orgs reach out because of this partnership?

**Mutual Success:**

Both parties report: "This partnership made us better at our work and deepened our understanding of how technology can honor relationships."
